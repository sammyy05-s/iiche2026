Acharya Prafulla Chandra Roy
Founding Figure · Chemist · Nationalist

Long before this department existed, A.P.C. Roy was already the reason Indian chemistry had a school to call its own. He discovered mercurous nitrite, built the country's first indigenous research lab in chemistry, and in 1901 founded Bengal Chemical & Pharmaceuticals — India's first pharmaceutical company — almost entirely on his own income.

He believed that a nation gets free by building things, not by collecting degrees. So when the Bengal Technical Institute needed someone to lend a fledgling chemical engineering course credibility and conviction in 1921, he was there.

“I have no sense of success on any large scale on things achieved … but have the sense of having worked, and having found happiness in doing so.”— Life and Experiences of a Bengali Chemist (1932), Roy's own autobiography. He wrote this after a life spent trying to pull his countrymen out of poverty — founding Bengal Chemical to create jobs, organizing famine and flood relief in Bengal for decades, and pushing indigenous industry as the way out of colonial economic ruin.

Where It Began

Built by visionaries,
carried by generations

In 1921, Prof. Hira Lal Roy and Prof. Baneswar Das stood in front of a class that didn't exist anywhere else in the country and started teaching chemical engineering. With Acharya P.C. Roy's backing, that first course at the Bengal Technical Institute — the predecessor of Jadavpur University — became India's first, and one of the oldest chemical engineering departments anywhere in the world.

The decades that followed weren't quiet ones. The department grew up alongside the National Council of Education's push for an education system built by Indians, for Indians, and later lived through the upheaval of the freedom movement and everything that came after it. Through all of it, classes kept happening.

When the Government of West Bengal enacted legislation establishing Jadavpur University on 24th December 1955, the National Council of Education handed over its institutions — this department among them — to the new university, where it has stayed and grown ever since.

A Timeline

One hundred years,
marked in milestones

From a single course at a technical institute to a century-old institution with alumni scattered across the world — the moments that shaped it, in order.

1921
The Department is Founded
A course in chemical engineering begins at the Bengal Technical Institute under Prof. H.L. Roy and Prof. Baneswar Das, backed by Acharya P.C. Roy — India's first.
1947
I.I.Ch.E. is Founded
The Indian Institute of Chemical Engineers is founded on 18th May 1947 — days before independence — with this department at its heart.
1951
First Degrees Conferred
The first Chemical Engineering degrees are awarded — the department's earliest batch of graduates.
1955
Jadavpur University is Established
By an Act of the West Bengal legislature, the NCE's institutions — and this department — become part of the newly formed Jadavpur University.
1966
First PhD (Engineering)
The department confers its first doctoral degree in engineering.
1970
A Department in the Doldrums
The Naxalite movement reaches the campus; classes are disrupted ,students organised .
1978
The Department Steadies Itself
Course reform resumes through the 1970s — Process Instrumentation and Control, Plant Design, Polymer Engineering — and the degree returns to a 4-year structure after 10+2 schooling.
1982
Diamond Jubilee
Sixty years of the department are marked and celebrated with alumni across generations.
1996
Platinum Jubilee
Seventy-five years of continuous teaching and research is commemorated.
2021–23
The Centenary
The department turns 100 in 2021; its Centenary Celebration Programme, 'Chemervescence', is held on 25–27 February 2023, reuniting alumni from across the globe and raising ₹46 lakh for the department.

The People Who Taught Them

Teachers who quietly
built a department

Every alumnus on this page started out sitting in front of a teacher whose name rarely makes the news. A few of the people who shaped this department across a hundred years — the current faculty roster follows below.

Prof. Hira Lal Roy & Prof. Baneswar Das
Founding Faculty, 1921
Built the department's very first course of study from nothing, alongside Acharya P.C. Roy. There was no syllabus to inherit — they wrote the first one.
Decades of Faculty
1940s–1970s
The names behind the changing syllabus rarely got written down, but their choices did: Chemical Engineering Thermodynamics arrived in the 1950s, Applied Kinetics by the late 1950s, and Process Instrumentation & Control, Plant Design and Polymer Engineering through the 1970s. Every one of those decisions was made by a professor standing in front of a blackboard, trying to keep the department current.
Departmental Leadership, 2023
Centenary Celebration Programme, 25–27 February 2023
The department's leadership at the time worked with alumni bodies to get the century-old departmental building repaired in time for the Centenary event, 'Chemervescence'.
Prof. Chiranjib Bhattacharjee
Alumnus & Vice-Chancellor, Jadavpur University (2026)
Trained in this very department before rising to lead the university itself, and has spoken often about how much the department owes to alumni who stay connected to it.

Where They Are Now

Alumni leading industry,
research, and each other

A century of graduates has carried this department's name into laboratories, refineries and boardrooms worldwide. Every entry below is tied to a moment in the department's own timeline — the batch they belonged to, or the year they were recognised.

Mr. Ravi Prasad
Batch of 1971
Former CEO, Produits Artistiques Métaux Précieux (PAMP), Dubai
Retired from running one of the world's leading gold and silver refiners, then came back and funded a ₹31-lakh chemical reactor for the department's teaching lab — ahead of the Centenary, 2023.
Prof. Arup K. SenGupta
Batch of 1973
P.C. Rossin Professor Emeritus, Lehigh University, USA
Left Jadavpur with a chemical engineering degree and spent a career on one hard problem: arsenic in drinking water. His polymer-based filter now protects millions of people, work recognised with the National Academy of Engineering's Grainger Prize. As of 2026 he remains an active researcher at Lehigh, recently extending his arsenic-removal sorbent work into direct air capture of CO2.

The Department Today

Still the department
that started it all

This is India's oldest chemical engineering department, and one of the oldest anywhere in the world. It currently offers a 4-year Bachelor of Engineering (intake 82), a 2-year Master of Engineering (intake 38), and, since 2011, a 2-year Master of Bioprocessing Engineering (intake 10), alongside PhD research. Active research spans Advanced Process Engineering, Bioprocess and Bioenergy, Membrane-Based Processes, Fuel Cells, Nanomaterials, Catalysis and Reaction Engineering, Environmental Pollution and Waste Valorization, among other areas, backed by UGC-SAP, DST-PURSE, MHRD and TEQIP funding.

Its labs today include the Momentum Transfer & Mechanical Operation Laboratory, Process Control Lab, Reaction Engineering Lab, Energy Engineering Lab, Bioprocess Lab, and analytical facilities under UGC-SAP (DRS) and DST-FIST, alongside a dedicated workshop, computer labs, drawing rooms, seminar rooms and a departmental library.

The I.I.Ch.E. Jadavpur University Students' Chapter is the current generation's way of holding onto the department's purpose — keeping its traditions, alumni network and shared history alive for every batch that comes through, because a department this old can very easily forget its own story if nobody keeps telling it.

The Students' Chapter

Carried forward by whoever's here now

The I.I.Ch.E. JU Students' Chapter does what every generation before it has done — keeps the department's community, its history and its ambitions moving. Practically, that means alumni events and departmental traditions. Quietly, it also means making sure a hundred years of story doesn't just fade out.
